## Anti-patterns verdict

**LLM assessment: pass, with two tells.** Nobody would look at this and say "AI made that." The photography is specific and real, the palette is a committed choice rather than a hedge, the type is Georgia and Arial rather than the reflex-reject families, and there is no gradient text, no hero-metric block, no fabricated urgency. The tells are (a) four near-identical card grids — `.hub-grid`, `.feature-grid`, `.product-grid`, `.giftcert-strip`, all the same border, radius, fill and shadow — and (b) two tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` over `index.html` + `assets/site.css` returns `[]` — zero findings, exit 0.

**Visual overlays:** not injected. The Browser pane stopped compositing frames mid-session, so screenshot and overlay presentation were unavailable. Evidence for Assessment B is instead direct DOM/geometry/pixel measurement at 1280, 760 and 375px, which is stronger than an overlay for the findings that matter here.

## Overall impression

This is a well-made page carrying an under-specified job. Craft is high — real photographs, a coherent warm palette, a documented design lock, a ~50-script gate chain. What it does not do is behave like a page whose purpose is to get someone to drive to a farm.

The single biggest opportunity: **the page never answers "can I come, when, and what will it be like?"** No hours. No season. No price near the top. No review from anyone who has been. No phone number until the footer. A visitor's real question is answered nowhere on the page they land on, and the first thing below the hero is a restatement of the navigation.

## What's working

1. **The hero photograph and its alt text.** A host, a guest, a child and an alpaca mid-walk — it shows the product rather than describing it, and the alt text ("A Tapestry Acres host and guest walking with an alpaca and child") is written in the brand's voice rather than as a filename.
2. **The failure states.** "Experiences are taking a pasture break. Please visit the experiences page to see what is available." Plain language, in voice, with a next step. Most sites ship a spinner that never resolves.
3. **The restraint in the palette.** `pasture-ochre` is committed — warm cream ground, forest green for headings, one terracotta accent reserved for action. Nothing hedges. The accent is never spent on decoration.

## Priority issues

### [P0] The mobile menu is dead

**Why it matters:** Two click handlers are bound to the burger — one inline in `index.html:73-75`, one in the build-injected `assets/site-shell.js`. Each tap toggles `aria-expanded` twice, so it never leaves `"false"`. Verified live at 375px. Below 700px the burger is the *only* navigation, so every phone visitor who lands on the home page can reach exactly one other place: whatever is linked in the body copy. For a farm whose traffic is overwhelmingly mobile and local, this is a revenue bug, not a UI bug.
**Fix:** Delete the inline script from `index.html` and `rv-rentals.html`. `site-shell.js` already handles Escape and click-outside.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Nothing on the page tells a visitor whether they can actually come

**Why it matters:** This is a destination business. The three questions every visitor has — *are you open, what does it cost, how long does it take* — are answered nowhere above the fold, and hours and season are answered nowhere on the page at all. The repo has `docs/visit-faq.md`; none of it ships. A visitor who cannot resolve "can I come Saturday?" leaves and does not come back.
**Fix:** A visit-facts strip directly under the hero — season/hours, price-from, duration, and "book ahead / walk-ins" — plus a tap-to-call. Replace the hub grid's slot with it; the hub grid is currently duplicating the nav in the most valuable space on the page.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify` then `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] Zero social proof

**Why it matters:** brand.md's conversion rules put real testimonials and real faces at the top of measured impact — the source case moved cost-per-lead from $200 to $30. This page has none: no review, no rating, no quote, no visitor photo credited to a visitor. For a stranger deciding whether to bring their kids to a farm they have never heard of, the absence is the loudest thing on the page. The site's `authenticity-audit.mjs` correctly forbids inventing any, which means the fix is editorial, not technical: pull real Google reviews.
**Fix:** Three real, attributed reviews between the herd teaser and the story section. Never synthesized — real names, real text, or nothing.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout` (once Steve supplies the reviews)

### [P1] The first thing below the hero is the navigation, again

**Why it matters:** "Plan your visit" is four cards linking to Experiences, Shop, Meet the Herd and RV Rentals — the same four destinations already in the header, 20px above. Measured at 375px it occupies **901px**, more than a full phone screen, to say what the nav already said. Combined with the hero's two CTAs and the header's six links, a visitor meets **twelve navigation choices before a single fact about the farm.** The cognitive-load checklist fails on single-focus, visual hierarchy, one-thing-at-a-time and minimal-choices — four failures, which is the "critical fix needed" band.
**Fix:** Cut the hub grid or demote it below the narrative. Give the space to visit facts and proof.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] The farm's story is told three times

**Why it matters:** "A working morning" (narrative), "Our story" (history), and "About Tapestry Acres" (summary) all say a version of *family farm, 110 acres, slower pace*. Three tellings dilute all three, and they push the contact details to the bottom of a 6,335px page.
**Fix:** Merge "About Tapestry Acres" into "Our story"; keep "Find us" as its own block and raise it.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

## Minor observations

- `state-skeletons.js` is loaded on every page and the home page's two async grids do not use it.
- The hero runs an 18-second Ken Burns loop forever, on a photograph that is already upscaled 1.5×.
- Gift certificates sit between the farm's story and its contact details — a low-intent product in a high-intent position.
- The `.field-label` eyebrow appears twice on the page; a third instance would make it section grammar rather than voice.
- Nav is six items; the working-memory ceiling is five. Folding "Animals" and "Meet the Herd" into one destination fixes both the count and the ambiguity.

## Questions to consider

- If a visitor could only read one screen before deciding, what should be on it? It is currently a headline and four navigation cards.
- What would this page look like if the phone number were the primary action rather than the last line?
- The farm has real visitors who had a real time. Why is none of that on the page?
- "Animals" and "Meet the Herd" are two doors to one room. Which one is the room?
