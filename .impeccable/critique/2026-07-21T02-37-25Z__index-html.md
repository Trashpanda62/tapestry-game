## Anti-patterns verdict

The site looks AI-assisted. The main tells are the cream/beige canvas, generic Georgia-style display serif, rounded cards with diffuse shadows, symmetrical section rhythm, decorative glyphs, and equal-weight card grids. It is competent but category-generic. Real farm photography, concrete acreage, and real products prevent it from feeling fake.

The deterministic scan reported six warnings across four rules: flat type hierarchy in `animals.html`; two apparent broken images; em-dash overuse in `meet-the-herd.html` and `rv-rentals.html`; and one dark-glow treatment. Both broken-image warnings are false positives because dialog images receive their sources at runtime. The dark-glow warning is localized. The typography and punctuation findings are useful polish signals.

Browser inspection found no horizontal overflow, no rendered broken images, and no console errors on Home, Experiences, Shop, or Animals at desktop and 375px mobile. No reliable live overlay was produced because mutable script injection was unavailable.

## Overall impression

Claude built a sound static-site foundation and completed difficult data work. The main opportunity is to replace the generic “premium rustic farm” wrapper with a Tapestry-specific story and a decisive conversion path. The current emotional journey is atmosphere → catalog. It should be welcome → confidence → booking.

## What is working

- The multipage architecture, shared navigation, Square checkout links, and full product catalog are substantive and useful.
- Real animal/farm photography gives the site credibility and warmth.
- The design is internally consistent, responsive, and technically stable.

## Priority issues

- **[P1] No primary conversion action above the fold.** The hero is a poster, not a decision point. Add one dominant “Book a farm experience” action, one quieter “Plan an overnight stay” action, and a compact trust line such as “By reservation · Monroe, Tennessee.” Suggested command: `/impeccable shape`.
- **[P1] Mobile hero consumes the entire first viewport.** Reduce the headline 25–35%, use natural wording without forced hyphenation, and fit the primary CTA and one trust signal before the first scroll. Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt`.
- **[P1] The information architecture reflects business inventory, not visitor intent.** Experiences, Animals, Meet the Herd, RV Rentals, and farm stay compete. Reframe top-level paths as Visit the Farm, Stay Overnight, Meet the Animals, and Shop the Farm; decide whether the herd game is content within Animals or a clearly named interactive feature. Suggested command: `/impeccable shape`.
- **[P2] The visual language is generic AI-rustic.** Replace beige-card sameness with an art direction rooted in this farm: family, working land, named animals, handmade fiber, Tennessee weather, and honest textures. Use fewer cards, more editorial image sequences, and a distinctive typographic voice. Suggested command: `/impeccable bolder`.
- **[P2] Technical accessibility and efficiency gaps remain.** Increase the 36×36 mobile menu and 36–42px controls to at least 44px, close the menu on Escape, implement keyboard behavior for shop tabs, and stop loading/parsing all 830 SKUs on the homepage just to show four products. Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`.

## Persona red flags

- **Jordan, first-time visitor:** cannot tell whether reservations are required, what to book first, where Monroe is relative to a recognizable destination, or whether children/walk-ins are welcome.
- **Riley, stress tester:** will find missing guidance around weather, animal unpredictability, cancellations, accessibility, safety, RV logistics, and arrival instructions.
- **Casey, mobile visitor:** sees an oversized headline, hidden 36px menu control, no visible CTA, and a long scroll before a useful choice.

## Minor observations

- “Highland cattle” and “farm stay” read more naturally without hyphens.
- “Shop yarn and farm goods” is clearer than “shop the fiber.”
- The empty-road hero is atmospheric but emotionally cold; a host/guest/animal interaction would better sell welcome.
- Orange-red links feel bolted onto the green/cream system.
- Product feed is about 760KB decoded; the homepage should consume a purpose-built featured subset.

## Questions to consider

- Is Tapestry primarily selling secluded beauty or the warmth of being welcomed into a working family farm?
- If the homepage could drive only one action, should it be Book a Visit or Plan a Stay?
- Should the site feel heritage-crafted, playful animal-led, or premium agritourism—and what real Tapestry behavior makes that choice true?
